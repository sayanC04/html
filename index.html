The Hybrid AI-Infrastructure Engineer Roadmap

From DevOps, Cloud or SRE at entry level, to Platform Engineer, to AI Infrastructure. Phase A (Stages 1–7) makes you employable. Phase B (Stages 8–12) builds platform and GPU/inference depth, ideally alongside a job. Take each stage's placement test first, and don't move on until you pass its checkpoint.

Rules for every stage

Placement test

Take it cold before starting. Clean pass: do only the project and break-fix quota. Partial: study only what you failed. Fail: full stage.

Checkpoint gate

Don't advance until you pass the checkpoint without notes. Record yourself explaining it and watch it back the next day.

Write it down

Every project gets a README, an architecture diagram, measured numbers, a failure log and a "what I'd change" section.

    If things go wrong

    If this happensDo this
    Fail the CKALog which domains cost you points, spend 3 weeks on only those, retake. Registration has typically included one free retake; confirm current terms.
    Stuck on a stage for twice its planned timeStop. Name the blocking concept, get help from a community or mentor, and learn the gap by building the project.
    Low callbacks (under about 5% after 50+ targeted applications)A résumé or targeting problem. Rewrite project bullets with measured numbers; aim more at GCCs and product companies.
    Callbacks, but failing technical screensWrite down every question you couldn't answer. Those are your next study topics.
    Final rounds, but no offersUsually communication or system design. More mock interviews; practice explaining your flagships out loud.
    No full-time job by month 18Take an adjacent entry role (cloud support, L1 SRE/NOC, Linux sysadmin, service-company DevOps trainee), keep doing Phase B, plan an internal move in 12–18 months.
    BurnoutMinimum-viable weeks for 2–4 weeks. Continuity matters more than pace.

    Quarterly market review

    Every 3 months, about 2 hours. This keeps the roadmap tied to your real local market instead of anyone's opinion.

    1. Collect 25 postings for your exact target title and locations (Kolkata, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, remote). Skip helpdesk roles labeled as DevOps.
    2. Record each one: role, company type (product, GCC, service, startup), location, skills listed, required vs nice-to-have.
    3. Count only required mentions.
    4. Adjust (a heuristic, not a law): anything required in roughly a third or more of postings that isn't Core or Important gets promoted. Anything Important that shows up in almost none across two consecutive reviews gets demoted.

    Roles you can target

    Apply confidently

    DevOps Engineer, Cloud Engineer (AWS), junior Site Reliability Engineer, junior Platform Engineer, Kubernetes / Cloud-Native Engineer.

    Internships from Stage 5, junior roles from Stage 6 (with CKA), broad applications from Stage 7.

    Stretch roles

    MLOps Engineer, ML Platform Engineer, junior AI Infrastructure Engineer, LLMOps / Inference Platform Engineer.

    Best odds at AI-native startups, or through an internal move after a year in a DevOps or SRE role.

    Not yet

    Senior or Staff Platform/SRE, GPU cluster / HPC engineer at scale, inference optimization engineer, Solutions Architect.

    These need years of production ownership or different skills (CUDA, InfiniBand, Slurm).

    Job-search keywords by market

    India

    "DevOps Engineer," "Cloud Engineer," "SRE" at product companies and GCCs. GCCs usually pay more than service companies for the same title.

    Lead your résumé with Kubernetes, Terraform and AWS. Filter out helpdesk roles labeled as DevOps.

    US

    "DevOps Engineer," "Cloud Engineer" as primary titles.

    "Platform Engineering," "ML Infrastructure," "AI Infrastructure" as résumé keywords.

    UK

    Same title strategy. Platform Engineering and DevSecOps are strong specializations.
